## Post 2: What's the best format to use on Reddit while commenting?

**Source:** [https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7473345129731432448/] 

**Full text (key excerpt):**
"What's the best format to use on Reddit while commenting?"

I get this question more often than you'd think, especially from folks who work at SaaS companies and want to be helpful on Reddit without coming across as salesy or promotional

And most of the time, the problem isn't intent but the format

Even when you're genuinely trying to add value, mention your tool because you actually use it, and disclose where you work, the way you structure your comment can still make you sound like you're selling something. That's why your comments get downvoted or removed by mods.

If the question is simple and someone wants a direct recommendation, don't write a paragraph. A quick no-fluff answer works best. Something like "I feel ya! Funnily, I work on the product team at BRAND, and we built X feature to solve literally this sooo annoying bug. After that, if you need X application, it might be worth checking out Y competitor, they have Z feature."

That's it. Be upfront, be brief, move on.

If the thread is seeking solutions, be helpful. If there's no immediate solution, be transparent and let them know when they can expect an update or whom they can reach out to.

If the original post lacks key details, ask a clarifying question before answering. It shows you actually care about helping, not just dropping your product name.

If there's a popular misconception in the thread about your category, a myth-busting comment works really well. You're not pitching, but correcting.

After running Reddit campaigns for B2B SaaS brands, we've narrowed it down to 8 comment formats that consistently work for half-disclosed employee accounts. Each one tells you when to use it and what it should look like.
